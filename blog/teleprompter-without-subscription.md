## What does "without a subscription" actually mean?

A no-subscription teleprompter normally uses one of three models: completely free, open source, or a one-time purchase. Those options are not interchangeable.

- **Free browser app:** nothing to buy, but browser security limits floating windows and screen-capture behavior.
- **Open-source desktop app:** no recurring payment, but installation and support may be less polished.
- **One-time native purchase:** pay once for the native workflow and keep using it without a monthly bill.

Some app-store listings advertise a free download but require a subscription after a short trial. That can still be a good product, but it is not what people mean when they search for a teleprompter app with no subscription.

## When the free web app is enough

The [free VoicePrompter web app](/web/) is the best option when you want voice scrolling without paying or installing anything. It runs in Chrome, Edge, and Safari, works as an installable PWA, stores scripts locally, and does not require an account.

Choose the web version for speeches, practice, simple camera recordings, or a second device placed near the lens. Choose a native app when the prompter must float over another app, record directly from the phone, or stay invisible during a Mac screen capture.

This distinction matters because many "free teleprompter" pages compare tools only by price. The real decision is whether the browser can support your recording setup. A free tool that cannot sit where you need it is more expensive in retakes than a native app you buy once.

## When CuePrompter is the simpler choice

CuePrompter is useful when you want a conventional fixed-speed prompter and do not need speech recognition. Paste the script, set the speed, and read. There is no account and no native installation.

Its limitation is pacing. If you pause, the text keeps moving unless you stop it manually. That is manageable for rehearsed speeches or an operator-controlled setup, but it is less natural for solo videos where the delivery changes between takes.

## When an open-source app makes sense

Textream is worth considering if you use a Mac, want source-code access, and are comfortable installing an unsigned app from GitHub. It attempts real voice tracking and can stay as a native window over other software.

The trade-off is operational. Installation requires more technical confidence than an App Store download, and its public issue tracker has included speech-tracking and language-reliability reports. Open source is valuable, but no subscription does not automatically mean less friction.

For a free and open-source browser approach, the VoicePrompter web app code is also public. The browser version is easier to start, while Textream provides a native Mac window.

## How to judge a no-subscription teleprompter

Test these five things before paying once for any app:

1. **Read off-script for ten seconds.** The prompter should wait instead of racing ahead.
2. **Skip a paragraph.** A real whole-script tracker should find the new position.
3. **Restart an earlier sentence.** Check whether it can scroll backward.
4. **Record or share the screen.** Confirm whether the prompt appears in the output.
5. **Close and reopen the app.** Make sure your scripts and settings behave as expected.

A Lifetime button is not the product. The recording workflow is the product. Run the same script through two or three options and choose the one that lets you stop thinking about the prompter.
